2026 competition robot

FRC Team 7415

I led the mechanical and code teams that built our 2026 robot, and I wrote over 90% of its code. This was my first ever major coding project, and I had to teach FRC based Java programming to myself from scratch.

Features:

  • Trapazoidal PID controller for linear intake
  • Finite state machine to determine robot states for ease of readability and changeability
  • Dynamic current limits based on current robot states
  • Bang Bang controller for the shooter, prioritizing spinup and recovery time
  • Capable of scoring from anyone mechanically possibe with regression and a lookup table
  • Four independently powered shooters each based on their own distance from the scoring location
  • Used vector subtraction and a time of flight lookup table to score while moving
  • Two cameras with Kalman filters working with drive odometry to get accurate robot pose down to the inch anywhere on the field
  • Internal automation so all robot functions can be prefomed with two buttons
  • Telemetry values logging motor values for post-match debugging

2026 Offseason Competition Robot

FRC Team 7415

An offseason robot built for the 2026 season. The team was given one month of limited meetings to fully design, program, and build the robot (instead of the typical 3 months). This meant that the robot had to be fully programmed before it was created physcially. Using physics based mechanism simulation, I was able to tune PID values before getting the robot, leading to no tuning needing to be done in real life.

Features:

  • Cycloidal intake to minimize slop
  • Finite state machine to determine robot states for ease of readability and changeability
  • Dynamic current limits to protect battery life
  • Accurate physics simulation of components to eliminate physical tuning
  • Uses motor current spike detection as a sensor to change robot behavior

2025 VCC Climber mech

FRC Team 7415

In the summer of 2025, My team did a offseason Cad challenge. My component, the climber, was a backpack climb based on FRC team 2056 in 2010. It consisted of a pair of complient wheels that would grip on and climb up a 1 inch by 1 inch bar. The competition judges said that it was their intended solution, and that not many teams figured out how to properly climb. To prototype, I built the design out of legos to ensure it would work.

Features:

  • 65% reduction to ensure the motor has enough torque to climb
  • gear and belt powertrain to ensure both wheels spin at the correct speed
  • small and compact frame
  • linear slides to encorperate with the rest of the robot

StellarXplorers National Space Design Competition

2025 to 2026

This NASA and Air and Space Forces Association sponsored competition gives teams a design prompt, such as building the most monetarily efficient satellite. As a core member of a 6-person team, I used Excel and Desmos to solve orbit planning, satellite component, and launch operations scenarios. I used excel to create parametric spreadsheets that allowed me to complete the semifinals round (usally requires 2-3 people) solo and got perfect points.

Features

Fully parametric spreadsheet that encorperates all design parameters, including environmental conditions, launch date, fines, and fuel consumption.

Auto calculated the score that I would get, meaning no manual calculation required.
